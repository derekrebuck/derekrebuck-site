About me

I’m usually the quiet one. The work is where the energy goes.

I’m analytical, reserved and pretty hard to read. Coworkers have jokingly called me Mr. Excitement, Mr. Energy and Mr. Expressive. The part that is not ironic is how much care I put into the work.

How my brain works

I tend to find structure before other people finish explaining the problem.

Someone can give me an abstract idea, a scattered brief or ten minutes of thinking out loud, and my brain starts sorting it into something concrete. I want to know what the organization does, who it needs to reach, why anyone should care, what the audience should feel, and what action should happen next.

I try to get into the head of both the client and the user. If those perspectives do not line up, that disconnect usually tells me where the real design problem is.

Once I understand that, I can sketch, test, question and refine. Sometimes I need several concepts. Sometimes I know the client well enough to just roll with it. Either way, the decisions have to hold up.

Design philosophy

Purpose before decoration.

If I put a circle on a page, I should be able to tell you why it is there.

What I believe

Good design does not ask the audience to do the designer’s job.

Make it work, then make it look right.

A beautiful design that functions badly is still a bad design. A functional design that looks terrible is not finished either. The best solution has to do both.

Accessibility is part of the design.

Low contrast, unreadable type, broken mobile layouts and inaccessible text are not small technical misses. They are signs that the audience was not considered carefully enough.

Show the passion through the work.

Skills can be taught. Pride in the work cannot. I would rather make fewer things at a high standard than lower the quality just to produce more.

Tools change

Fundamentals still matter.

I am curious about new tools, including AI, but I do not confuse speed with judgment. AI can be useful for framing ideas, testing directions and reducing administrative work. It is not a substitute for understanding the audience, making design decisions, or reviewing the final result.

I would rather learn how a tool can improve the process than pretend it does not exist. The standard for the finished work stays the same.
