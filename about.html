Intention and purpose are at the core of successful design.

Good design can stand on its own. If an element cannot be justified, it probably does not need to be there.

I’m Derek Rebuck, a multidisciplinary designer based in the DMV. Graphic design is my core foundation, with work that also extends into web, UI/UX, communications, video and data.

I’ve spent more than a decade working across visual design, websites, digital communications, video and data. Most of my career has involved moving between disciplines instead of staying in a single lane.

That range helps me focus on the problem before deciding on the format. The right answer might be a website, a visual system, an infographic, a campaign or something else entirely. I start by understanding what needs to be communicated, who needs to receive it and what the work ultimately needs to accomplish.

I also studied political science alongside graphic design, which strengthened the research, writing and analytical side of how I approach creative work.

01

Understand the context

Before I start designing, I need to understand both sides of the problem. Who is the audience? What needs to be communicated? What does the organization want people to understand, feel or do? Is the current approach actually accomplishing that?

02

Define the problem

I narrow the project down to the actual design problem. What is not working? What information matters most? Where is the disconnect between the message and the audience? A clear problem keeps the work focused on solving the right thing.

03

Build the structure

I organize the information before polishing it. That might mean establishing hierarchy, mapping a page, building a visual system or deciding how the content should behave across formats and screen sizes.

04

Design for real use

Accessibility, readability, mobile behavior, contrast and technical limitations are part of the design from the beginning, not a check at the end.

05

Refine with purpose

I review the work critically and remove what is not helping it. Every color, type choice, image and interaction should contribute to the message or the experience.

My experience spans federal service, health communications, the courts, real estate, manufacturing, education, startups and independent work. That mix has given me a broad view of how different audiences use information and how design needs to adapt to context.
