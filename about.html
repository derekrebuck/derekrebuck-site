Intent and purpose are key to successful design.

Good design should be able to stand on its own. If an element cannot be justified, it serves no purpose.

I’m Derek Rebuck, a multidisciplinary designer based in the DMV. Graphic design is my core foundation, but my work also extends into web, UI/UX, communications, video and data.

Why my range matters

I’ve spent more than a decade working across visual design, websites, digital communications, video and data. Most of my career has involved moving between disciplines rather than staying in a single lane.

That range helps me look beyond the obvious solution. Sometimes the answer is a website. Sometimes it is a visual system, an infographic, a campaign, a video or something entirely different. I start by understanding what needs to be communicated, who needs to receive it and what the work ultimately needs to accomplish.

I also studied political science alongside graphic design, which strengthened the research, writing and analytical side of how I approach creative work.

How I design

My process starts with understanding the problem before deciding what the solution should look like.

01 Understand the context

Before starting a project, I need to get in the head of both the client and the user. Who is the target audience? What needs to be communicated? What is the end goal? Is the current messaging actually achieving it?

02 Define the problem

Once I understand the context, I narrow it down to the actual design problem. What is not working? What information matters most? Where is the disconnect between the message and the audience? Defining that clearly keeps the project focused on solving the right problem.

03 Build the structure

Before worrying about polish, I establish the hierarchy and framework the design needs to succeed. That might mean organizing content, mapping a page, establishing a visual system or determining how information should flow across formats and screen sizes.

04 Design for real use

The design has to work outside of a mockup. Accessibility, readability, mobile behavior, contrast, technical limitations and real-world constraints are considered from the beginning, not added as a final check.

05 Refine with purpose

I review the work critically and remove anything that is not helping it. Every color, type choice, image, interaction and visual element should contribute to the message or the experience. If I cannot explain why something is there, I question whether it should be there at all.

Background

My design career began in 2013 with web and nonprofit work while I was still in college. Since then, much of my career has centered on public service and communication, with work spanning the federal courts, health communications, intellectual property, education, real estate, manufacturing, startups and independent projects.

Across those roles, I’ve worked in graphic design, web, communications, photography, video, analytics and front-end development. That range has shaped how I approach projects today: understand the problem first, then choose the right way to solve it.
