Design Principles
01

Purpose

Every element should have a reason to be there. If it is not supporting the message, hierarchy or experience, I question whether it belongs.

02

Accessibility

Accessibility is part of the design itself. It should shape choices from the first concept, not appear as a compliance check at the end.

03

Precision

Details matter. I review the work carefully because small inconsistencies can weaken an otherwise strong design or create unnecessary friction.

04

Adaptability

The solution should fit the audience and the problem. I am willing to change direction when the evidence shows there is a better way to make the work succeed.

Design Process
01

Understand the context

I start with the audience, the message and the goal. What do people need to know, feel or do, and is the current approach getting them there?

02

Define the problem

I identify what is unclear, what is missing and where the organization and audience are not connecting so I can solve the right problem.

03

Build the structure

I organize the information and establish hierarchy before polishing the visuals. A sound framework makes better design possible.

04

Design for real use

I account for accessibility, readability, responsive behavior and practical constraints while the design is taking shape.

05

Review and refine

I test the choices, review the details and remove what is not helping. The final work should feel considered without feeling overworked.

Creative Direction

Keep the pieces working together.

When a project stretches across multiple formats, I think about the overall visual direction as much as the individual deliverables. The goal is consistency without making every piece feel identical.

01

Set the visual tone

Establish the look, hierarchy and personality early enough that later decisions have a clear reference point.

02

Build coherence

Make sure typography, color, imagery and layout feel related even when the format or audience changes.

03

Adapt without losing identity

Give the system enough flexibility to solve new problems without drifting away from what makes it recognizable.

Information Design

Make the information easier to use.

Some projects are less about adding visual impact and more about making complicated material understandable. I look for the hierarchy, relationships and path through the information before deciding how it should look.

01

Find the hierarchy

Separate what matters most from what supports it so the audience knows where to begin.

02

Shape the path

Organize content so people can move through it naturally instead of having to decode the structure themselves.

03

Use visuals when they help

Charts, icons, diagrams and other visual tools should clarify the information, not decorate it.
